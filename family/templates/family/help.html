Workspaces, and switching between them

Each workspace has its own family tree, its own list of upcoming birthdays and yahrzeits, and its own notification settings, kept completely separate from any other workspace's. If you're part of more than one - most commonly, the family you were born into and the one you married into - a Switch Workspace link appears in the top navigation so you can move between them. Whichever one you're currently in is shown in the page title and the top left of every page. Nothing you do in one workspace affects another.

{% endif %}

Signing in

There are no passwords. Signing in sends a one-time link to your email or phone - open it and you're in. If you're ever signed out, just request a new link the same way.

The family tree

Open "View family tree" from anyone's page to see the interactive chart: drag to pan, scroll to zoom, click a card to re-center the tree on that person. A small icon on a card means there's more of the tree in that direction - click it to expand. A "Horizontal view" button switches the layout between generations stacked top-to-bottom (the default) and generations arranged left-to-right, useful for tracing one narrow branch. A dashed connector between two people means they're engaged but not yet married.

Your own notifications

My Notifications is where you manage what you personally hear about, and on which channel (email or SMS). For each event type you can choose to hear about everyone, just your immediate family (spouse, parents, children, siblings), or mute it entirely. On any specific person's own page, a button next to each event type lets you add a one-off exception for just that person - included even though you'd normally miss it, or excluded even though you'd normally hear about it. That exception always wins over your general setting.

Why some people don't show up in the list

The People list only shows people who are actually part of this family's day-to-day notifications. Some records exist purely so the family tree renders correctly - for example, an in-law's own parent, who isn't part of this family and doesn't need birthday or yahrzeit reminders. Those records are hidden from the list by default; you can still reach one by following the tree or a relation link from someone who is tracked.

Roles

Members can view the tree and manage their own notification subscriptions. Editors can additionally add and edit people and relationships. Only the owner can remove someone from the family or delete a marriage record - editors can't undo that.

Contact info and sign-in access

A person record and a login are two different things. Entering someone's email or phone on their profile is what actually gives them their own sign-in - before that, they're just a record in the tree with no way to log in themselves. The same login can represent someone in more than one family (their birth family and the one they married into), so setting contact info never creates a duplicate account for someone who already has one.

Hebrew and Gregorian dates

Every date in this app - birth, death, and marriage alike - is recorded in both calendars independently. Neither is computed from the other, because a birth, death, or wedding that happened after sunset has already advanced to the next Hebrew day - only whoever's entering the record actually knows the answer. Enter both if you know them; the Hebrew date is what actually drives scheduling (a birthday, yahrzeit, or anniversary is worked out fresh against the Hebrew calendar every year).

Two per-person settings handle calendar quirks that would otherwise make a date ambiguous in some years: which Adar a birth/death/marriage in Adar is observed in in a Hebrew leap year, and whether a date on the 30th of Cheshvan or Kislev moves to the 1st of the next month or the last day of the (now 29-day) month, in a year where that month is short. Defaults follow common custom; override them if a family's practice differs.

Marriages, engagements, and divorce

Add a spouse from either person's page. There's no separate "engaged" status to remember to change later - a marriage is recorded as married from the moment it's added, even if the wedding date is still in the future. While the date is still ahead, the tree shows a dashed connector and the couple gets a one-time "Wedding" reminder instead of a yearly "Anniversary" one; once the date passes, that switches over automatically.

Divorce and widowhood aren't automatic in the same way - nothing else in the record implies them, so update the marriage's status directly when one happens. That's also what stops the yearly anniversary reminder for a marriage that's ended.

A marriage record also has its own separate engagement date (both calendars, same as the wedding date) - editing a marriage shows an "Engagement" section above "Marriage" for it. It's what a couple gets a one-time "Engagement" reminder from, the same way a wedding date gets a one-time "Wedding" reminder. Unlike Wedding/Anniversary, though, the yearly "Engagement Anniversary" reminder doesn't stop once the couple actually marries - it keeps going indefinitely alongside the real anniversary, for as long as both spouses are still living and tracked.

Recording a death

Entering a death date is what marks someone as no longer living - there's no separate toggle for it. Once that's recorded, birthday (and Bar/Bat Mitzvah) notifications for that person stop, and yahrzeit notifications take over automatically. If they were married, their marriage's anniversary reminder stops too, the same way.

Bar and Bat Mitzvah

A boy's 13th Hebrew birthday, or a girl's 12th, automatically becomes a Bar or Bat Mitzvah notification instead of an ordinary birthday one - but only once the Hebrew birth year is entered, not just the month and day. Without it, there's no way to work out which birthday is the 13th (or 12th) without guessing, so it stays an ordinary birthday every year until the year is filled in.

In-laws and cross-family visibility

An in-law reached through a marriage is visible on both sides - you can see and follow a link to your child's spouse, even though they belong to a different family's workspace. You still can't edit anyone outside your own family, though; that visibility is read-only.

How notification settings are decided

For any given event, the most specific setting wins: a one-off exception on that particular person or marriage beats your own general setting for that event type, which beats the family's default for it if you've never touched it. "Immediate family" always means spouse, parent, child, or sibling - that definition doesn't change per family.

Site admins

A site admin isn't a role within your family - it's someone who helps run Family Tree itself, across every family using it, similar to how a phone carrier's staff aren't part of your household but can still help set up your line. There's no self-service way to create a new workspace yet (see above), so a site admin is who sets one up, and who adds the first person to it. They can also see every family's still-pending broadcasts, not just their own, as a safety net, and are who to contact to change a workspace's SMS sender name or reply-to email - owners and editors can see those settings but can't change them themselves. Most families never need to think about this day to day - it only comes up if you're told to contact one, or someone tells you they're setting one up for you.

Broadcasts

A broadcast is a one-off, free-text update - a Broadcasts link appears in the navigation for owners and editors. It goes out to everyone in the family who hasn't muted broadcasts (from My Notifications, the same as any other event type), at the time you choose - queued to go out right away by default (it can take a few minutes to actually send), or scheduled for later. You can optionally tie it to one or more people (an engagement, a new baby, a couple's wedding details) so it's clear what it's about; anyone subscribed to "immediate family only" hears about it if they're immediate family of any of those people.

Unlike other event types, a broadcast has no per-person exception - muting or subscribing applies to all of them at once. It also isn't computed the way a birthday or yahrzeit is, so it never appears on the Upcoming page - the Broadcasts page is where you track it. A broadcast you scheduled can still be edited or cancelled right up until it sends; once it's gone out, it's kept as history but can't be changed. A still-pending (not yet sent) broadcast only shows up there for whoever created it - other owners'/editors' pending broadcasts stay invisible to you until they've actually gone out, at which point they become visible to everyone with access, as a shared record. A site admin (see above) is the one exception - they can see every pending broadcast, not just their own.

Previewing a notification

On the Upcoming page, owners and editors see a small Preview button next to each entry, showing exactly what its email and SMS will say - subject line included. It's shown as it will actually read on the day it sends, not as of right now, which matters for something still weeks out - a birthday ten days away reads "is on Monday" once it's actually about to send, not the far-off date it'd show if you looked at it today.

When the notification's own send date differs from the event's date, the preview shows both, so it's clear why: a Shabbos/Yom Tov shift moves a notification a day or more earlier (nobody's expected to check their phone once Shabbos or Yom Tov has begun), and a Wedding is always sent a few days ahead of the day itself on purpose. Previewing never sends anything or changes the real occurrence - it's read-only.

The History section

Near the bottom of a person's page, owners and editors can see a running history of changes to that person and their relationships - who changed what, and when. It's there for catching mistakes (like a birth date entered wrong) and understanding how a record got to its current state.
